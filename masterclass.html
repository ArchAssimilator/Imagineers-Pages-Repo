The Argument

Four things you do not know about AI

Day one is built on four claims. Almost no executive has heard any of them (800+ will attest), and each one changes what you get back from every prompt you write from here on. Open any of them to see how we teach it.

☉ The whole of day one, on one board If you only look at one thing on this page, look at this.

The four claims are a single causal chain. The labs ration your output, so structure is how you prove you are worth more. Structure gets you adult answers, but business-grade answers need the expert method. And even then context rot is waiting, sooner and harder than you have ever been told.

1 You are being treated like a school kid Your answers are being rationed, and it is an economics problem, not a technology problem.

Every major lab is underwater on inference (AI output): a dollar of revenue against two dollars of cost. Roughly 95% of users pay nothing at all, and of the 5% who do, about half the queries are trivial.

There is only one available response, and it is triage. Fewer tokens, split thinly across reading your input, thinking, and writing the output, sent to cheaper models. The model is not being lazy with you. It is behaving like a rationing system, not a tutor.

Once you accept that you are on the wrong side of a rationing decision, the fix stops being "find a better AI" and becomes "prove you are not a trivial query", which is what everything that follows does.

The rest of day one is about getting you out of the cheap queue.

2 You can get adult results with simple prompt structure RCTIO. Five parts, about ten seconds, a visibly different class of answer.

The fastest available upgrade is to show the model that a serious adult is on the other end and that slop will be sent back. We use RCTIO: Role, Context, Task, Instructions, Output. Not new, not clever, and after 45 courses still the most dependable framework we have found.

Two things happen. The model spends more on you, often two to four times more, because it can see the answer is going to be checked and bounced. More importantly, the structure forces you to say what you actually want, which is usually the real problem. It is also the largest single lever we have found on hallucination: most confident wrong answers are the model filling a gap you left open.

You do not have to write it by hand. The exercise everyone remembers is handing the model your scruffy intent prompt, asking it to rebuild that as a full RCTIO prompt, then running the rebuilt version in a clean chat. The gap between the two answers is where the room goes quiet.

The carwash example settles it in ten seconds. "Wash my car. The carwash is 100m away. Walk or drive?" returns "walk, it is closer, and here's why...": confident, useless, and the wrong goal. Same question, now with structure, and it tells you to drive, because the car has to be at the wash. The prompt was the weak link, not the model.

Structure supplies the context the model cannot guess. The cheapest quality upgrade available, and it works on every model you will ever use.

3 But business-grade results require expert methods Amateurs jump to an answer. Experts run a method, then triangulate.

Adult results are still not business-grade, and the gap is method. Ask one prompt for a board-ready answer, and the token economics from claim one guarantee something diluted and insipid: plausible, fluent, and not worth taking to a decision.

So we break it up. First, ask what methodologies a genuine expert would consider, each with credible provenance. Second, rank them for your specific issue, with a reason you could defend. Third, execute the chosen one as a named process, step by step, all done by the AI but under your guidance.

Each step is small enough to be done properly at the token budget you are given. The compound output is work you can take into a board meeting. This is the biggest step change delegates report, and it is why day one is not a prompting course.

Run a method rather than jumping to an answer: several deliberate steps beat one clever prompt, every time.

4 And you will still walk into context rot We measure how fast it happens. The numbers are worse than anyone expects.

Real business information is not a list of facts, it is a mesh of connected ones: sources, entities, constraints, causes, timelines, evidence. As context grows, the connections weaken and drift. The facts are all still in there, but the relationships between them fray.

MRCR-8 is the closest approximation of that mesh, and the results are sobering. At 16k tokens, roughly 20 pages of input, the leading models are already shedding accuracy fast. This despite 1M context windows. Non-thinking modes fall off a cliff. Bigger context windows do not fix this and are actively deceptive about it, because more irrelevant context makes it worse rather than better.

So the last stretch of day one is diagnostic. Seven ways context breaks, the business symptom each one produces, and the action that prevents it. Once you can name the failure mode, you stop blaming the model and reaching for a different one.

Then the countermeasure, which is a filing habit rather than a clever prompt. Convert source documents to Markdown, because a Word file can be a thousand times the size of the same text, and you pay for that out of the context you needed. Work in one numbered file per stage, so output is saved, and the output becomes the next input. And when a long chat drifts, ask for a handoff: a self-contained summary of every decision taken and option rejected, which you paste into a clean chat.

It sounds administrative. It is the difference between AI work you can hand to a colleague, a client or an auditor, and AI work nobody, including you, can reconstruct a week later.

Structure your prompts, run expert methods, and reset often. Context rot is the failure mode that ruins otherwise good AI work.

The Objection

"Our IT department will not let us put company data into AI."

It comes up in every room, and it is why capable executives are still stuck on the wrong side of the 80% wall, so we deal with it properly rather than waving it through.

We give this guidance from inside a military-grade cyber intelligence firm, one of the businesses RJ co-founded, and from the vendor side of enterprise software, where he ran one of the first ten companies in the world licensed to resell Microsoft Copilot. That combination is why the guidance is specific rather than cautious.

The uncomfortable part is that your documents are already syncing to somebody else's servers, and nobody agonised over that decision. So we do not run an ethics panel. We give you a working position. What should never be uploaded, what to redact, which settings to switch off on day one, which jurisdictions to keep off company work and why, and a policy your auditors can tick without stopping the business, and then we get back to the work.

Security, company data and the IT conversation

This is the block delegates most often say they did not expect and most often say they needed. It is delivered from the perspective of a military-grade cyber intelligence firm, it is practical rather than theoretical, and it covers personal security as well as company data, because in practice the two failures are connected.

  • Two-factor authentication everywhere, using an authenticator app or a hardware key rather than SMS, and the SIM-swap case study that explains why.
  • Device hygiene, including the argument for keeping banking off the phone you carry.
  • What actually happens to the files you upload, per vendor, and which privacy and training settings to change before you do anything else.
  • The legal frameworks behind certain models that compel retention and disclosure of everything typed into them, and why that is a long-horizon business risk rather than a today risk.
  • A redaction standard for client, patient, employee and personal data that staff will follow, as opposed to one that looks good in a policy document.
  • How to write a one-page AI data policy that satisfies an audit without freezing the organisation, and how to have the conversation with an IT function that has already standardised on one vendor.

We are opinionated here and we say so. Both facilitators have commercial history on the vendor side of this argument, so you get a position rather than a warning.

The working system: Markdown, staged files and handoffs

Good prompting collapses without a place to keep the work. Day one therefore builds a small, boring system alongside the prompting, and most delegates report it is the habit that survives longest after the course.

  • Convert everything to Markdown. Rich formats carry enormous overhead. A Word file can be orders of magnitude larger than the same text, and you pay for that out of the context you needed for thinking. We cover which conversion tools are currently reliable for PDFs, slides and spreadsheets, and how to check the conversion worked.
  • One file per stage, numbered. Input saved, output saved, output becomes the next input. It reads like admin until the first time somebody asks you how a conclusion was reached.
  • Handoff prompts. When a conversation starts to drift, you ask for a self-contained summary of decisions taken and options rejected, then open a clean chat with it, which is the practical answer to context rot.
  • A governance trail. The numbered files become a reviewable record of how a piece of AI work was produced, which is what makes AI output safe to delegate inside a team or hand to a client.

We use Obsidian for this because it is free and it renders Markdown properly, though any text editor will do the same job.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need prior AI experience?
No. The course starts with fundamentals and moves quickly into practical, business-grade implementation. Complete beginners and daily users sit in the same room and both leave with more than they expected.

Will this be relevant to my specific industry?
Yes. The frameworks are cross-industry by design, and both formats include guided translation into your own context. Private cohorts are built around your sector directly.

How many people are in each session?
Twelve, always. It is capped so that both facilitators can get to every person's actual work.

What is the difference between the two formats?
Tailor-Made is private and customised for one organisation. Open Enrolment is the Daily Maverick Executive AI Masterclass: public, cross-industry, delivered with them and booked through them. The core curriculum is the same.

Do you sell consulting after the training?
No. We sell no platform, no licence and no implementation engagement waiting on the other side of the two days, and we are booked out delivering the course in any case. The implementation happens inside it. Ten of the thirteen modules close with every delegate writing down one specific thing they will do with it in their first week back, dated and captured in the room. A private team course of fifteen delegates walks out with about 112 of those, printed as one document for whoever signed off the two days.

Our IT department has standardised on one AI tool. Is the course still useful?
Yes, and it is often more useful. We work across the major platforms because they are not interchangeable, and part of the value is being able to have an informed conversation with your IT function about what your current stack does well and where a second tool would be worth the licence.

Can we use real company data in the exercises?
That is your call, and we help you make it properly. We cover what should never be uploaded, what to redact, which settings to change first, and how to write a data policy that satisfies an audit without stopping the business. Delegates who prefer to work on anonymised or public material lose nothing.

Which model should I be using?
It depends on the job, and it changes every few weeks. More importantly, the model is only half the story: the surrounding toolset determines whether a platform can convert your file, run the calculation or build the deck. We show you how to tell the difference and how to keep testing it after the course.

Do you deliver outside South Africa?
Yes. Private cohorts are delivered internationally on request, quoted on enquiry.

What should I bring?
A laptop and pre-configured accounts on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot or Grok. At least one paid subscription is required. We send setup guidance beforehand.

What happens after the two days?
First Wednesday. On the first Wednesday of every month at 3pm we run a one-hour Zoom session for alumni: what changed in the past month, where it lands in business, and a sophisticated AI tool or method handed over live. It is included for every delegate, indefinitely, and it is how people who did the course two years ago are still current.

Is there a certificate?
No. You leave with working prompts, workflows and outputs instead. We have found that senior delegates value the second thing considerably more.
