The Difference

The consultant leaves you a roadmap.
We leave you a running system.

Most AI programmes fail in the budget meeting, before anybody writes a line of code. They get scoped the way IT was always scoped: one large system, one large number, a business case that pays back in eighteen months, and an executive sponsor whose credibility is now attached to a single launch. Nothing about that shape holds up against a technology that changes every quarter.

So the pilot succeeds, because pilots are designed to, and then it meets real data, real staff, real procurement and real edge cases, and stops being mentioned. Nobody is at fault, because nobody was ever in the building.

Forward deployment closes that gap by refusing to open it. We work in your environment, on your data, against the constraint that is costing you money, from the first week. What we build is narrow on purpose, because narrow things survive contact with an organisation and grand ones do not.

We train, we advise and we build, and each one makes the other two sharper. The 725 executives we have taught show us which use cases pay. The board mandates show us which ones survive funding and governance. Both land in what we build, which is why our scoping is short and why what we leave behind tends to still be running a year later. Whatever we build runs on models you already pay for, and you own it outright when we go.

The Playbook

Start where it is embarrassingly easy

The first thing we build for you should be almost too small to be interesting. Cheap, quick, no risk, and returning something absurd against what it cost. Those wins are what buy the right to attempt the hard ones.

Day One

The wins nobody has to approve

Work that costs a few hours and a subscription you already pay for, and hands back a day a week. No procurement, no integration, nothing to defend if it fails. There are always more of these than anyone expects, and they are usually sitting in the same three departments.

The Compounding

Early wins are political capital

Each one buys the next. A finance team that got its month-end back does not need persuading about the next build, it starts asking. That is how a programme travels from curiosity to mandate without a single steering committee.

Later

The hard build, with the organisation behind it

By the time we reach the work that is complex and expensive, it is no longer a bet. The value has been proven in the same business, by the same people, on the same data. The money becomes easy to find and the resistance has already been spent.

The Inversion

Small first is not slow

The organisations moving fastest on AI are not the ones that spent the most upfront. They are the ones that got something working in week one and never stopped shipping. Starting small is the quickest route to large. Starting large is usually the slowest route to nothing.

The failure rate you have read about is not a fact about AI. It is a fact about how AI keeps being bought.

Insource the Outsourced

The biggest prize in a large business is the work it pays somebody else to do.

Every large organisation has a ring of vendors around it, doing work that went out of the door because doing it in-house was expensive. Document processing. First-line support. Research and monitoring. Report production. Transcription and translation. Basic design and copy. Parts of legal review, parts of recruitment, parts of finance operations. All of it left for the same reason: internal headcount was the costly way to get it done.

That calculation has inverted. Much of that work can now be run inside the business, by people already on the payroll, with a designed pipeline carrying the volume, for a saving that is real without being the whole of it.

What it does to jobs is the bigger story. Most staff hear AI and hear redundancy. Insourcing lets you tell the opposite story, and tell it truthfully: the work is coming back, and your people are the ones who will run it. It is the only version of this conversation we have seen that makes an organisation faster and less frightened at the same time.

The window is open, and it is not open indefinitely. Your vendors are automating the same work. When they finish, they keep the margin and offer you a price cut instead of a capability. This advantage belongs to large organisations, which is unusual, but only to the ones that move first.

  • The budget already existsIt is sitting in vendor invoices, not in a new line item that has to be argued for.
  • The work is already specifiedAnything outsourced has a contract, a spec and a volume history. That is most of the scoping done before we arrive.
  • The people are already thereThe capacity to take the work back is on the payroll, which is exactly what turns this into a jobs story rather than a headcount story.
  • The margin is worth havingYou were paying a vendor's overhead and profit on work a pipeline now does at cost. That gap funds everything else on the roadmap.
How It Runs

Five stages, and the first one is not engineering

We keep engagements short and visible. Once we start building, if we cannot show you something working inside a week, the problem is either the wrong one or not ready, and we would rather find that out on day five than in month four.

Alignment

First, get the leadership to a shared understanding

The Masterclass, run privately for your executive team. Leaders who know what the models really do scope better problems, kill the wrong ones before they cost anything, and stop asking for things that cannot be built. Alignment at the top is what makes the next four stages fast.

This is the stage most engineering firms cannot offer, and the one every failed AI programme skipped. If your leadership has already done it, we start at week zero. Most have not. See the Masterclass.

Week 0

Two days inside the business

Not a discovery workshop. We sit with the people whose time is being consumed and watch the work happen. We arrive already knowing the failure patterns, because we have watched 725 executives hit the same ones.

Week 1

The first working artifact

Something real to use by day five. It will be narrower than you hoped and more useful than you expected. It exists to prove the shape of the problem, and to kill the idea early if the shape is wrong.

Weeks 2 to 6

Build, and hand over while building

Your people work alongside ours from the start. Every stage is a numbered file, every decision is recorded, and the whole thing leaves a governance trail your auditors and your successors can follow.

After

We leave, it keeps running

Your team owns and operates it. We stay available for the next problem rather than embedded on a permanent retainer. An engagement that cannot end is dependency dressed as engineering.

One honest caveat, because it comes up in every scoping conversation. Sometimes the right answer is that AI should not be doing this job at all, or not yet, or not on this data. We will say so, and say why. That answer is worth more to you than another engagement is to us.

What We Build

Pipelines and tools, not platforms

Almost everything that pays commercially today is a designed sequence of steps, each small enough to be done properly. Unglamorous, and where the money is. A representative sample of the work. Why pipelines, not agents?

Repeating Work

Proposals, tenders, recruiting, board reporting, competitor and market intelligence. Designed once, run by anyone, with output consistent enough to govern.

Analysis

Knowledge
