The Terms

Seventeen words worth knowing exactly

Ordered by how often they come up in a real leadership conversation, not alphabetically. Four of them have a full explainer behind them.

Context rot

Context rot is the degradation of an AI model's output as the context it is working with grows. The facts stay present, but the relationships between them weaken and drift, so the model produces fluent answers that are quietly wrong. Bigger context windows do not fix it. Full explainer: what is context rot.

Business-grade output, and school-grade output

Business-grade output is AI work you would put your name on and send to a board: sourced, specific, in your house format, and defensible under challenge. School-grade output is fluent, generic and correct-sounding, which is the default a model gives an untrained user. The gap between the two is a skill, not a model upgrade.

AI literacy versus AI fluency

AI literacy is knowing what the technology is and what it can broadly do. AI fluency is being able to get a specific piece of your own work done to a standard, with a model, unaided. Most corporate AI programmes buy literacy and then report it as fluency.

Structured prompting

Structured prompting is writing a request with its parts named and separated: the role, the material, the task, the constraints and the output format. It is the difference between asking a question and issuing a brief, and it is the first thing that moves output up a grade.

Expert-method prompting

Expert-method prompting is telling a model which professional method to apply, rather than only what answer you want. Naming the method a good analyst would actually use raises the output far more than any amount of extra politeness or insistence in the request.

Context management

Context management is the discipline of deciding what to put in front of a model and what to keep out, so the material it works from stays small, current and relevant. It is the highest-leverage habit an executive can build, and it is the direct defence against context rot.

Context window

The context window is the maximum amount of text a model can hold in front of it at once, counted in tokens. A large advertised window is a capacity limit, not a promise of quality at that size. Models lose accuracy long before they run out of window.

Token

A token is the unit a model reads and writes in, roughly three quarters of an English word. Context limits and API pricing are both measured in tokens, so it is the unit that quietly governs both quality and cost.

Hallucination

A hallucination is a confident, fluent statement from an AI model that is not true. It is not a bug waiting to be patched out; it is a property of how these models work. That is why verification has to be designed into the process rather than bolted on at the end.

System prompt

A system prompt is the standing instruction that sits above every conversation, setting the role, the rules and the house style. Writing one good system prompt is worth more than rewriting the same request a hundred times.

AI pipeline

An AI pipeline is a fixed sequence of steps where a model does the thinking at each step, but a human or a script decides the order. Almost everything that pays commercially today is a pipeline rather than an agent. Full explainer: AI pipelines versus agents.

AI agent

An AI agent is a system where the model itself decides what to do next and in what order, rather than following a sequence someone laid out in advance. That freedom is what makes agents powerful, and what makes them hard to hold to a standard. Full explainer: AI pipelines versus agents.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

Retrieval-augmented generation is fetching relevant documents from your own material and putting them in front of the model before it answers, so the answer is grounded in your data rather than the model's memory. Most "AI trained on our documents" products are this.

Frontier model

A frontier model is one of the handful of most capable general models available at a given moment. The set changes every few months, which is why capability judgements have a short shelf life and policies written against a named model age badly.

Fractional CAiO

A fractional CAiO is a Chief AI Officer who works on a retained or standing-mandate basis rather than as a full-time employee. The accountability is the same as a permanent CAiO; the employment relationship is not. Full explainer: what is a fractional CAiO.

Forward deployed engineer (FDE)

A forward deployed engineer works inside a client's business, in their environment and on their own data, shipping working systems against the real problem, rather than handing over a roadmap and leaving. Full explainer: what is a forward deployed engineer.

Chief AI Officer (CAiO)

A Chief AI Officer owns the organisation's AI agenda at board level: what to build, what not to buy, what to deliberately wait on, and the data and risk position underneath all of it. The seat is the same whether it is filled full-time or fractionally. How we run the mandate.

In Practice

Definitions are the cheap part

Knowing what context rot is takes a minute. Recognising it in your own output, and knowing what to do about it before you send the document, is what the two days are for.
