## C1. What actually changed for 725 executives

**Original data. Nobody else can write this. Highest long-term value.**

The firm has run 45 two-day courses. Nobody has published what came out of them.
This is the piece that would make Imagineers.ai the source an engine reaches for
rather than one of several providers it lists.

What it needs: the aggregate, anonymised patterns from the cohorts. What
executives could not do on day one and could do by the end of day two. Which of
the twelve use cases lands hardest and which one people actually go back to
work and use. The distribution of starting skill, not the average.

Information gain: total. There is no comparable dataset published in South
Africa, and probably not anywhere.

Effort: real. It needs the numbers pulled and checked before a word is written.
Do not write it from memory, because the entire value is that it is measured.

Blocked on: someone deciding what is safe to publish and pulling the figures.

## C2. The financial model in Excel, start to finish

**Documented methodology. Second highest value.**

The course builds a full financial model in Excel from a plain-English brief. It
is named on the site and never shown. A page that walks the whole thing, with
the actual prompts and the actual output, is the kind of artefact that gets
quoted and linked, because it proves a claim instead of making one.

Information gain: high. Plenty of people say AI can build a model. Almost nobody
shows the brief, the prompts and the working file.

The obvious objection is that it gives away the course. It does not. Reading how
a model was built is not the same as being able to do it under time pressure on
your own numbers, which is what people pay for. The publish-the-method firms
tend to win the lane.

Effort: a day, mostly screenshots and copy.

## C3. The honest guide to AI executive training in South Africa

**Listicle format. Fastest citation, shortest half-life.**

A comparison of the providers in this market, named, with the criteria stated up
front, and Imagineers.ai included rather than centred. This is the page that
gets pulled into "best AI training in South Africa" answers.

Providers to cover: GIBS, The Knowledge Academy, College Africa Group, School of
IT, Prospen Africa, DB23, and Imagineers.ai.

Suggested criteria, published before the table so the method is visible:

- Who is in the room teaching, and what have they run
- Format and length, and how much of it is hands-on
- Whether delegates work on their own material or on case studies
- Seniority the course is pitched at
- Published price, or no published price
- Cities, and whether in-house delivery is offered

**Deliberately not drafted.** Every row makes a factual claim about a named
competitor, and getting one wrong is both unfair and reputationally expensive.
Each provider's offer has to be checked from their own published material, with
the date it was checked recorded, before this page goes live. An agent inventing
these rows would be the single worst thing in this backlog.

Two rules if it does get written: be genuinely fair, including where a
competitor is the better answer for a given buyer, and re-check it every six
months or take it down. A stale comparison page is worse than none.

Also worth saying plainly: this format is a tactic. It buys inclusion quickly
and decays as everyone writes one. C1 and C2 are what actually hold the lane.

## C4. When not to send your exec team on an AI course

**Contrarian take. Cheap, and disproportionately quotable.**

Straight argument for the cases where training is the wrong spend: no
executive sponsor, no permitted tools, a policy that forbids putting real work
into a model, a team that will not get time to practise afterwards.

Information gain: high, because it argues against the firm's own product, which
is rare enough that engines and humans both remember it. It also fits the
existing tone, where the answer to "should we buy this AI tool" is usually no.

Effort: half a day. It needs no new data.

## C6. The AI policy an auditor will pass

**Practical artefact for the board ICP.**

The CAiO page mentions setting the data and security position. The board ICP has
almost no consideration-stage content aimed at it, which is the thinnest part of
the current coverage. A publishable template, with the reasoning for each
clause, would fix that.

Information gain: medium-high. Templates are common; templates with the
reasoning attached, from someone who has actually had to defend one, are not.

Effort: a day.

## The requirement every item above has to meet

No page joins this site unless it carries at least one of:

- The firm's own data
- The firm's own results
- A documented method, shown rather than described
- A named case with real numbers
- A take that argues against the consensus and says why

A page that only restates what already ranks adds nothing an engine can use, and
publishing it dilutes the eight pages that do carry something. If a proposed
piece cannot name which of the five it has, it does not get written.
